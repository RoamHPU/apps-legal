This is the privacy policy for FileSnap Docs by Roam ("FileSnap Docs", "we", "us"), an iPhone application published by Roam HPU.

The short version: we do not collect any personal data. FileSnap Docs scans and reads your documents on your iPhone, and never uploads them. There is no FileSnap Docs account, no FileSnap Docs server, no analytics SDK, and no advertising network embedded in the app. Document records and their text stay on your iPhone, and in your iCloud Backup if it's on. So do your scans, unless you pick a save folder in the app's Settings, such as one in iCloud Drive. Any expiry reminder you add goes into your default calendar, with the document's title, type and expiry date, and syncs wherever that calendar does.

The rest of this document explains that in detail.

1. Information we do not collect

We do not collect, transmit, store, sell, or share any of the following:

  • Personal information (name, email, address, phone, identifiers)
  • Account information (because we do not have user accounts)
  • Document images, OCR transcripts, issuer names, document types, tags, notes, expiry dates, or any other content you create or capture in the app
  • Photos from your photo library beyond the moment you choose to import a document
  • Camera frames beyond the moment you capture a document page
  • Calendar events beyond the moment you opt in to creating one
  • Device identifiers (IDFA, IDFV, advertising IDs)
  • Crash reports, analytics events, telemetry, usage statistics
  • Diagnostic logs

There is no remote endpoint that FileSnap Docs connects to for any of the above.

If you have turned on Share With App Developers in iOS Settings, Apple may share crash data and statistics about how you use the app with us, under Apple's own privacy policy.

2. Information the app stores, and where

FileSnap Docs creates and stores the following information, and never sends any of it to us:

  • Document images and PDFs: saved in the app's private storage, or in the save folder you pick (see below).
  • Document metadata: title, issuer, type, date, expiry date, tags, notes, OCR transcript, page count and file format, saved in the app's local SwiftData store.
  • Settings: your filename pattern preference, save location and other preferences, saved in standard UserDefaults.
  • Calendar events (optional): when you opt in to an expiry reminder, FileSnap Docs creates an event in your iOS Calendar via Apple's EventKit framework. The event lives in your calendar, not on our servers (we don't have any).
  • iPhone search index: so you can find documents from iPhone search, the app adds each document's title, type, issuer, tags and dates to your iPhone's search index. It also adds whether you marked it personal, business or tax-deductible, the first 500 characters of its text and a small preview of its first page. The app removes a document's entry when you delete the document. To stop this, turn off Show Content in Search for FileSnap Docs in iOS Settings.

By default, your scans are kept on your iPhone, in the app's private storage, which the Files app can't show. If you have iCloud Backup enabled for your iPhone in iOS Settings, this data may be included in your iCloud Backup, which is governed by Apple's iCloud terms and privacy policy.

FileSnap Docs has no iCloud or CloudKit sync of its own. If you pick a save folder in the app's Settings, for example in iCloud Drive, your scans are saved there. So are your encrypted exports and a log of any bulk renames (old and new file names). That service stores and syncs them under its own terms. When you pick a folder, JPG scans kept in private storage are copied into it. PDFs, exports and anything in a folder you picked before stay where they were.

Each scan also carries a copy of the details you saved it with, inside the file. These include its title, issuer, type, dates and tags, whether you marked it personal or business and tax-deductible, and your iPhone's language and region setting. They go wherever the file goes, and anyone you give the file to can read them with common photo or PDF tools. This can't be switched off, and editing the document later doesn't update the copy. If you turn on Stamp QR with metadata in the app's Settings, the same details are also stamped on the first page as a QR code. Document records, their text and your settings stay on your iPhone.

3. Permissions we ask for and why

iOS permission Why we ask What we do with it
Camera To capture a document when you tap "Add Document", then "Take Photo". We pass each captured page to Apple's on-device Vision framework for OCR. The scan is then saved in the app's private storage on your iPhone, or in the save folder you pick, such as one in iCloud Drive (see section 2). FileSnap Docs never uploads the scan, but a folder in a service such as iCloud Drive is synced by that service.
Photos To import an existing photo of a document. We read only the photo you pick. We do not enumerate, scan, or upload your photo library.
Calendar (optional) To create an expiry reminder when you ask for one. We add one all-day event to your default calendar, 7, 14 or 30 days before the expiry date (you choose), with an alert at 9 am. It shows the document's title, type and expiry date. iOS asks for full calendar access. We use it only to add that event, and to remove it if you remove the reminder or delete the document. We do not read your other events, contacts or invitees. If other people can see that calendar, such as a shared family or work calendar, they can see the reminder too.

You can revoke any of these permissions in iOS Settings at any time. The app will continue to work with reduced auto-fill convenience.

4. Required reason API declarations

iOS requires apps to declare why they use certain "required-reason" APIs. FileSnap Docs declares:

  • UserDefaults — to persist your settings on your device. (Reason CA92.1)
  • File timestamps — to sort and display your documents by date. (Reason C617.1)

Both are local-only uses. The full declarations are in the app's PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy manifest, which is reviewable by Apple at submission.

5. Encrypted export

When you use FileSnap Docs's encrypted ZIP export to bundle documents for sharing:

  • The encryption happens on your device.
  • The password you choose is not transmitted, stored, or known to us. We never see it. If you forget it, we cannot recover it.
  • The result is a standard .zip file. Each file inside it is encrypted with AES-256, using the WinZip AES method.
  • The names of the files and folders inside it are not encrypted. They include your document titles, so anyone with the .zip can read them without the password.
  • The .zip is saved in your save folder, and stays there after you share it. In the app's private storage, it stays until you delete the app. You share it with the iOS share sheet, and where it goes (email, Files app, AirDrop, third-party app) is up to you.

6. Third parties

FileSnap Docs does not embed:

  • Advertising SDKs
  • Analytics SDKs (no Firebase, no Mixpanel, no Amplitude, no nothing)
  • Crash reporters (no Sentry, no Crashlytics)
  • A/B testing SDKs
  • Social network SDKs

FileSnap Docs uses Apple's first-party frameworks (Vision, SwiftUI, SwiftData, PDFKit, EventKit, Core Spotlight, StoreKit, Translation, Foundation). They run on your device, with two exceptions: StoreKit asks the App Store for purchase details, and an expiry reminder syncs wherever your calendar does. If you tap Translate, Apple's translation feature handles the text, and it may first download a language from Apple. Apple's frameworks are governed by Apple's privacy policy, not ours.

7. Children

FileSnap Docs is rated 4+ and is suitable for general audiences. We do not knowingly collect data from children, because the app collects no data from anyone.

8. Your rights

Because the app sends us none of your data, there is no copy we hold to provide, correct, or delete. Your documents and records stay on your iPhone, or in the folder you chose, and they are yours.

To delete FileSnap Docs's data, delete the app from your iPhone. Deleting the app removes the files kept in its own folder, along with your document records and settings. Files in a folder you chose, such as one in iCloud Drive, stay there until you delete them. Calendar events you created via expiry reminders remain in your iOS Calendar and can be deleted there separately.

To export your data: use FileSnap Docs's built-in share / export action, or restore an iCloud or Finder/iTunes backup to a new device.

If you email us, we receive your email address, your message and anything you attach. Please black out anything sensitive before you attach a document.

If you have a question about your data, email ben.lewis@roamhpu.co.
